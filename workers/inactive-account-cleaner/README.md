# Inactive-account cleanup and saved deletion recovery

This draft replaces the old Firebase-first, best-effort cleanup. All environments default to `INACTIVITY_MODE="audit"`. The hourly schedule, configuration and migration 028 are not deployed. Do not enable execution until the remaining recovery/reconciliation and external-writer checks in `docs/account-deletion-recovery.md` pass.

An audit reads at most five database candidates and reports `next_user_id`; pass `afterUserId` to inspect the next audit page. It does not call Firebase, Stripe or Resend, mutate KV, claim operations, set warning flags or advance the stored cursor. A database candidate is not verified deletion eligibility.

Execution resumes at most five saved requests and inspects at most five new candidates. Pending requests retain their stored user/inactivity origin. Active or uncertain operation claims and crashed execution tokens are excluded, never expired. Storage-only recovery can finish without provider credentials. Pending records rotate behind other queued work; unscoped candidate pages advance with a revision check and wrap. Scoped tests always start at the selected account.

New candidate work holds exclusive account maintenance, checks fresh Firebase and D1 activity and reads Stripe without mutations. The worker never repairs plan labels. After 23 months of inactivity it can stage a warning. A notice is marked accepted only after a provider receipt and an atomic receipt/activity-flag transaction. These receipts prove API acceptance, not inbox delivery or reading. The email states that cleanup may occur after 24 months, no sooner than 30 days after notice. Missing addresses are never marked warned. Recent activity invalidates an earlier warning cycle.

Warnings use a stable Resend idempotency key and identical payload for up to three definitively rejected attempts. Network errors, malformed successful responses, 408/409/429/5xx, and receipt-persistence failures retain uncertainty without automatic resend. Crashed sends remain `sending`; uncertain sends remain `needs_review`, with their operation claim retained. Resend keys expire after 24 hours, so elapsed time alone never authorizes another send. Known rejections that exhaust retries require review but do not block ordinary account activity.

After 24 months, a matching accepted notice at least 30 days old and fresh eligibility checks permit creation of a durable inactivity intent. The worker releases its own maintenance claim before invoking the shared processor. Billing verification, identity reconciliation and content erasure use that processor; automatic cleanup never cancels subscriptions or expires checkouts. Completion notification is staged atomically, then dispatched by the separate outbox pass on a later invocation. Delivery failure never reopens completed erasure.

Execution requires an explicit `INACTIVITY_MODE="execute"`. Development and QA additionally require `INACTIVITY_TEST_UID` for one disposable account. Dev and QA currently select the same Firebase project in `js/firebase-config.js`; do not use an account needed in either environment. QA's reserved Firebase configuration is not its selected configuration. Project-pinned credentials, the matching Stripe test/live mode, D1/KV bindings, and environment-specific `FRONTEND_URL` must be verified before activation. Warning and completion delivery also require `RESEND_API_KEY`. No public HTTP endpoint runs cleanup.

Remaining operational checks include actual scheduled QA query/subrequest bounds, full audit pagination, uncertain/crashed-claim reconciliation, independent Firebase sign-in/Stripe-write coordination, completion-notification QA and disposable-account recovery. Failed maintenance or an ambiguous send can hold the account until reconciled; this draft must not activate without that recovery path.

References checked September 20, 2026: [Resend idempotency keys](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/idempotency-keys), [Cloudflare Workers best practices](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/best-practices/workers-best-practices/).

Structured batch logs contain counts and cursor positions, not addresses, Firebase UIDs, provider bodies or credentials. The scheduled handler rejects incomplete or newly uncertain batches so runtime monitoring can identify a failed run; it does not report an ambiguous send as success. This is an observable runtime failure, not a claim that an owner-notification integration has been configured.

The completion outbox runs before recovery and selects at most five pending completed jobs per invocation. Dispatch requires both the job and matching admission to be complete, and owns a durable execution token before contacting Resend. A versioned, fixed payload uses `account-deletion/<job-id>` as its idempotency key. Definite rejections retry after one and four hours, at most three attempts; uncertain results and failed receipt writes never authorize another send. An accepted receipt immediately clears the address.

Unsent addresses become eligible for redaction after seven days; each execution redacts at most 100 expired addresses and reports any remaining backlog. It also purges at most 100 sent/expired, address-free notification receipts older than 90 days. These limits require a functioning schedule and backlog monitoring; they are not a hard deletion deadline. Operational job/admission receipts and anti-abuse tombstones are preserved. Retention does not take over a sending token, and a late receipt cannot restore a redacted address. Audit reads this state without dispatching or changing retention.
