What to expect from a JobHackAI Voice Mock Interview

What happens before, during, and after your practice interview.

JobHackAI·September 29, 2026

A JobHackAI Voice Mock Interview gives you a way to practice answering questions aloud for the role you want. Choose a target role and seniority, speak with an AI interviewer, and review feedback after the session.

A session can run for up to 20 minutes. Your first lifetime interview is free and includes a feedback preview. Paid access adds the full report, transcript, and more practice within your plan or pack allowance.

1. Choose your role

Start with the role you are preparing for and the relevant seniority level. Add a job description if you have a particular opening in mind. It gives the interview more context about the responsibilities and skills in the posting.

If you do not have a job description, you can still practice with the role and seniority. Pick a role that reflects the interview ahead of you so your examples have a useful frame.

2. Answer aloud

When you start, allow microphone access and answer the AI interviewer out loud. Explain what happened, what you personally did, and what came next. Use details you can support from your own work, studies, or projects.

Try answering from a few prompts instead of reading a memorized script. You may notice that an example that looked clear in your notes needs more explanation when you say it aloud.

For a short preparation exercise, try a 45 to 75 second introduction, then adjust it to the question. That timing is a practice constraint, not a scoring rule. The guide to answering “Tell me about yourself” includes two fictional examples and a way to build your own outline.

3. Handle follow-up questions

The interviewer may ask for more detail based on your answer. You might need to clarify a decision, explain your part in a project, or describe a result.

Treat the follow-up as another chance to make your experience clear. If you mostly described what the team did, explain your own contribution. If you named an outcome, connect it to the action you took. You do not need to invent a number to make an answer useful.

When you are finished, use the end control or say that you would like to end the interview. The session then moves to feedback.

4. Review your feedback

Start with one thing to work on. You may need a clearer structure, a more specific example, or a better explanation of how your experience connects to the role.

The free interview includes a preview with one strength and one area to improve. It lets you try the conversation and see the kind of feedback available. Full reports and transcripts require paid access.

What the paid report adds

The full report includes feedback on communication, answer structure, content depth, and role fit. It also provides an answer-content breakdown, coaching, and a transcript so you can review what you actually said.

A very short interview may be unscored because there is not enough material to assess. Give the interviewer enough complete answers to review, and treat the feedback as preparation guidance based on that session.

Read the transcript alongside the coaching. Find a place where you could explain a decision or result more clearly, then try that answer again using the same facts. Let your wording change while keeping your experience accurate.
