# Sales Development Representative Interview Questions

SDR interviews are auditions more than interrogations. The interviewer is simulating a prospect the entire time: do you listen more than you talk, do you recover from interruptions, do you sound like someone a stranger would give 90 seconds to. Expect a live roleplay, usually a cold call or objection drill, and expect to be cut off mid-sentence on purpose.

The behavioral questions look standard but score differently than other roles. Numbers rule everything: activity volume, connect rates, meetings booked, show rates, quota attainment by month. Coachability is the other axis, and they test it live by giving you feedback during the roleplay and watching whether you actually apply it on the second attempt.

Study the questions, learn the two worked answers, then drill out loud daily. For this role more than any other, voice practice is not optional prep, it is the literal skill being purchased.

## The 12 questions to prepare for

### 1\. Sell me our product. You have two minutes.

What they are really asking: Preparation and structure under pressure: did you research, and can you run discovery before pitching.

How to answer: Open with one sharp discovery question instead of features. A pitch that starts with a question about their world beats a memorized monologue every time.

### 2\. Cold call me. I am a busy VP who did not expect this call.

What they are really asking: The actual job, live: pattern interrupt, permission, relevance, and close for time.

How to answer: Acknowledge the interruption, earn 30 seconds with a specific relevance hook, ask one question, close for a meeting, not a sale. Expect and welcome the brush-off.

### 3\. How do you handle hearing no fifty times a day?

What they are really asking: Rejection durability, the trait that predicts SDR survival past month three.

How to answer: Get concrete: your reset ritual, how you mine nos for pattern feedback, and the metric mindset (every no is progress through the funnel math).

### 4\. Tell me about a month you missed quota.

What they are really asking: Honesty plus diagnosis: misses happen, and they want your debugging behavior.

How to answer: Own it without excuses, show the diagnosis (activity, connect rate, or conversion), the one change you made, and the next month's number.

### 5\. Walk me through your prospecting process for a new account.

What they are really asking: Whether you do targeted research or spray generic sequences.

How to answer: Show the 5 minute research ritual: trigger event, persona pain hypothesis, personalized first line, and a multichannel cadence with specific day spacing.

### 6\. What do you say to 'just send me an email'?

What they are really asking: Objection handling on the most common brush-off in the job.

How to answer: Agree, then trade: 'happy to, so the email is not junk, can I ask one question about how you handle X today?' Convert the deflection into discovery.

### 7\. How do you use AI tools in your outbound, and where do you not?

What they are really asking: 2026 reality: AI drafts everything, so they are checking your judgment about what still needs a human.

How to answer: AI for research summaries, draft personalization, and call notes; you for the first line, the call itself, and anything a prospect would smell as generated.

### 8\. How do you prioritize your account list on a Monday morning?

What they are really asking: Pipeline math instinct: time is the only inventory an SDR has.

How to answer: Tiers by fit and intent signals, hot replies first, then time-boxed call blocks. Mention protecting two daily call blocks from email drift.

### 9\. Tell me about the best piece of coaching you ever received.

What they are really asking: Coachability, the number one screening trait for early career sales.

How to answer: Name the specific behavior change (talk less, pause after questions), who gave it, and the measurable difference it made. Then show you still seek feedback.

### 10\. What is your current quota and where are you against it?

What they are really asking: Comfort with accountability and numbers fluency.

How to answer: Answer instantly and precisely: quota, attainment by month, and your funnel rates. Hesitation on your own numbers is the red flag.

### 11\. Why sales, and why now?

What they are really asking: Motivation durability for a job with daily rejection.

How to answer: Tie it to something real (you like measurable wins, you want income tied to effort) and show you understand the grind, not just the commission stories.

### 12\. What questions do you have for us?

What they are really asking: Whether you evaluate them like a seller: territory, leads, ramp, and what good looks like.

How to answer: Ask about ramp expectations by month, inbound versus outbound mix, SDR to AE promotion record, and what their top rep does differently.

## Two worked sample answers

### Tell me about a month you missed quota.

Last March I finished at 70 percent of my meetings target, eight booked against a quota of twelve. Instead of blaming the territory, I pulled my own funnel apart with my manager. Activity was fine, about 80 dials and 120 emails a day, but my connect to meeting conversion had dropped from 12 percent to 6. Listening back to call recordings, the problem was obvious: I had started pitching features in the first 30 seconds instead of asking about their current process, because I was anxious about the slow month and rushing.

I rebuilt my opener around one discovery question and forced a two second pause after it. April came back at 11 meetings, and May was my best month that quarter at 14. The miss taught me to debug conversion before increasing volume, because doubling a broken motion just produces more rejection at scale.

**Why this works:** Owns the number in the first sentence, diagnoses with funnel math instead of excuses, finds the behavioral root cause in recordings, and shows recovery with specific numbers. The closing principle proves the lesson generalized.

### What do you say to 'just send me an email'?

I treat it as a polite no that can become a real maybe. On the call it sounds like this: 'Absolutely, I will send it right after this. So it is actually useful and not another template in your inbox, can I ask one thing: how is your team handling outbound personalization today, tools or by hand?' About half answer, and the moment they answer, it is a conversation again, and I can earn a second question or close for fifteen minutes.

If they hold the line, I respect it, send a three sentence email referencing exactly what they said, and call back two days later with that email as my opener: 'I am the one who sent the note about your team doing personalization by hand.' That callback converts better than my cold dials because the context already exists. The principle: never argue with the brush-off, trade compliance for one question.

**Why this works:** Gives actual call language rather than theory, shows the trade technique, has a realistic fallback that converts the loss into a warmer second touch, and quantifies behavior. Sounding like a real call is exactly what this question is scored on.

## Sales Development Representative interview FAQ

### Will they really make me roleplay a cold call?

At most companies, yes, and often twice: once cold, then again after feedback to test coachability. The second attempt matters more than the first. Apply the feedback visibly, even if it feels awkward.

### What numbers should I memorize before the interview?

Your quota and attainment by month, daily activity volume, connect rate, meeting conversion, and show rate. If you are new to sales, know the numbers from any measurable thing you have done: deliveries, fundraising calls, retail upsells.

### I have no sales experience. What do I lean on?

Evidence of rejection tolerance and competitiveness: sports, door knocking, service jobs, cold applying. Then prove hunger by doing the job in the interview: research the company, build a ten account target list, and bring it.

### How fast does this role lead to Account Executive?

Typical paths run 12 to 24 months. Ask directly: how many SDRs were promoted last year, and what was the criteria. A team that cannot answer has no path, which matters for your decision.

### What is the best way to practice cold calls without burning real prospects?

Drill out loud against something that pushes back. A voice mock interview can play the busy VP, interrupt you, and throw objections, so your first stumbles happen in practice instead of on your highest value accounts.
