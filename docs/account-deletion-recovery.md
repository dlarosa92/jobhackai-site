# Durable deletion recovery — integration in progress

Inactivity integration prerequisites now include a separate, read-only `assertInactiveBillingClear` guard. Unlike explicit account deletion, it rejects every nonterminal subscription and open Checkout instead of canceling or expiring them. It shares the ownership, pagination, payment, invoice and schedule checks with the deletion guard. Failed or ambiguous evidence does not establish eligibility. The replacement inactive-account worker now invokes this helper under exclusive maintenance admission; the scan itself is not a lock.

The project-pinned Firebase client can now fetch a fresh, minimal activity projection, validating `lastLoginAt` as epoch milliseconds and `lastRefreshAt` as RFC3339. Missing timestamps remain unknown; a missing identity is a distinct result. Malformed timestamps, invalid calendars, foreign users/tenants and a malformed `users:null` response fail closed. The latter previously could be mistaken for an absent identity. No provider account data besides the two timestamps is returned. Field semantics were checked against [Google's UserInfo reference](https://docs.cloud.google.com/identity-platform/docs/reference/rest/v1/UserInfo).

Deletion admission now requires an explicit stored origin (`user_request` or `inactivity`). Only the authenticated delete route sets `requestedByUser:true`; a background invocation must find an existing intent and cannot create one from an arbitrary UID. A verified user request may upgrade a pending inactivity intent without changing its reference; automated retries cannot downgrade that consent. Completed intent provenance remains unchanged.

The durable processor honors inactivity origin with read-only billing and fresh provider/D1 activity checks before billing and again before identity removal. Eligibility requires two years of known inactivity, no current subscription or unexpired pack entitlement, and a matching accepted warning for the current address at least 30 days earlier. Any activity after that warning invalidates it. Migration 028 now stages warning-delivery records; legacy timestamps alone do not establish acceptance. The replacement worker now stages and sends these warnings with durable receipts and uncertainty handling; it has not been deployed.

If the account is no longer eligible, the processor confirms identity presence and atomically withdraws the automatic intent/manifest, retaining a minimal reason receipt and restoring access. Billing failures likewise withdraw automatic cleanup when identity is freshly confirmed present, without provider mutations. Withdrawal requires the owning execution token, quiescence and a pre-removal phase; a concurrent explicit user request makes the transaction fail rather than discarding consent. Ambiguous identity state stays pending for reconciliation. Confirmed remote deletion resumes storage cleanup without repeating billing mutations, and final erasure removes the warning address/receipt.

The legacy inactive-account worker and unused Firebase deletion helper have been replaced. The new worker creates intents under maintenance admission, sends accepted-warning notices and resumes bounded saved requests through this processor. Uncertain-operation reconciliation and external identity/billing coordination remain unfinished; completion delivery is implemented in the draft and still requires deployed QA. Fresh reads alone do not close the final race with an independent Firebase sign-in or external Stripe write. This work does not authorize or enable automatic deletion, and migration 028 remains unapplied.

The prior handler could remove Firebase sign-in and then fail to delete stored content or KV payloads without a durable retry path. This draft now wires the authenticated delete endpoint to durable intent, manifest preparation, exclusive execution, verified provider phases and retryable cleanup. API and signed-webhook admission are wired. Bounded scheduled recovery and the known worker admission paths are now connected in the draft. Live-session handling, uncertainty reconciliation and deployed completion-notification QA remain unfinished, so this is not a completed release. Migration 028 remains unapplied and must precede deployment.

Migration 028 creates a job outside the users foreign-key tree. Its initial snapshot contains the verified UID, original D1 user ID, notification email and KV keys. It deliberately stores no bearer token, Firebase service credential, resume text, interview transcript or scorecard. Preparing the job must succeed before billing or identity side effects.

The storage helper has four phases: prepared, billing_verified, identity_removed and complete. Only the caller that has actually confirmed billing and Firebase results may advance it. A crash or timeout during identity deletion leaves billing_verified; recovery must reconcile the identity instead of inferring success. This phase record does not itself perform those provider operations or certify their outcome.

For an identity-confirmed job, cleanup first makes its storage-key manifest durable and writes the authoritative deletion tombstone. It deletes each KV key without swallowing errors. A failed key leaves every D1 content reference and the job available for retry. A single D1 batch then removes content, the account, consent-owned campaign links and queued Analytics delivery, and records completion. A failed statement rolls back the entire batch. Payment/refund/ledger records remain financial history. Completion clears the recovery email and storage paths; the operational UID receipt and existing fraud-prevention tombstone remain.

The final transaction checks UID/user-ID ownership again; a changed owner rolls back erasure. This is defense against stale recovery data, not an application-wide write lock. New requests, pending Checkouts and webhook races still require the integration below.

## Durable delete endpoint and identity reconciliation wired in the draft

The authenticated POST handler now takes the UID only from the verified Firebase token, creates a durable deletion intent, waits for admitted work, snapshots references, and invokes the recovery processor. A job uses the same reference as its intent. The original best-effort post-identity erasure path and bearer-token deletion fallback have been removed from this endpoint. A successful provider operation never substitutes for the completed storage transaction.

The processor validates its configured Firebase service account against FIREBASE_PROJECT_ID before accepting a new intent. It imports the signing key locally, uses project-scoped Admin calls with the verified UID, and keeps credentials/tokens in memory only. Billing must be confirmed before identity removal. A fresh identity lookup follows every delete attempt, including errors/timeouts. A retry in billing_verified first checks whether identity is already absent; if so, it resumes cleanup without repeating billing or identity mutations. If identity still exists, billing is checked again before deletion. Provider payloads and diagnostics are never returned to the user.

Execution uses a durable exclusive token. Concurrent retries cannot both run providers. Normal completion or a handled pending result releases only its own token; a crash or failed release leaves it for explicit reconciliation. No age or lease timeout grants permission to take over. The future runner/reconciliation path must address interrupted executions before this can be promoted. Identity-removed storage retries need no Firebase/Stripe credentials, and completed jobs return their receipt without contacting providers.

HTTP 200 means the content erasure transaction completed. HTTP 202 means the request is saved but unfinished, with a reference and a coarse explanation; it does not assert completion or automatic recovery. Identity removal can be confirmed true, confirmed false, or unknown. The account UI keeps the local session and offers Check Deletion Status while removal is unconfirmed. It signs out only after confirmed removal and explicitly surfaces pending storage cleanup. Unexpected failures return a generic 503 rather than internal SQL/provider errors.

## Retention uses exclusive account maintenance

Retention now selects a bounded page of accounts and acquires an exclusive maintenance claim for each account before any erasure. Admission is atomic: earlier account/billing/Analytics operations prevent maintenance, and an active or uncertain maintenance claim prevents new operations for that UID. Other accounts continue normally. Deletion requested first excludes the account; a deletion requested during maintenance waits for its storage work and settlement. Signed webhooks return a retryable failure while maintenance is busy, rather than recording a paid event as a processed no-op. Analytics defers its event for retry, and the follow-up worker skips the busy account.

Every retention mutation is scoped using the actual owner column: numeric users IDs for ordinary tool/voice tables, Firebase UIDs for LinkedIn/cover-letter history, and the parent resume for feedback. KV payload removal happens before deletion of its D1 references. At most ten resume payloads per account are selected; only those selected IDs can be erased from D1 in that pass. Remaining payload work is reported explicitly. The existing 90-day rules, pinned/recent/reused-content exclusions and locked latest free voice-history metadata are preserved.

Each invocation processes at most 25 accounts. Migration 028 adds a revisioned cursor so later invocations continue and wrap; a slower overlapping invocation cannot overwrite a newer cursor. The draft cron is hourly instead of daily to work through these bounded pages. All environment modes remain `audit`; nothing has been deployed or enabled. Audit makes no claims, cursor writes or KV mutations, reports its selected range and next cursor, and accepts an explicit `afterUserId` for read-only pagination. One batch is not a whole-database retention audit. Unmapped UID-owned legacy records are counted for ownership reconciliation, not silently ignored or erased under an invented owner; that count must be checked in deployment preflight.

A partial failure does not advance the cursor. The account retains an uncertain claim (or an active claim if settlement fails) and its resume references for explicit recovery; no timeout releases maintenance automatically. This deliberately means a failed maintenance operation can hold that account's requests until reconciled. The recovery/reconciliation path remains a release requirement, and this draft must not deploy without it. The replacement inactive-account worker now uses the same maintenance admission; uncertainty reconciliation remains required.

Tests cover both operation orderings, cross-account isolation, deletion during payload cleanup, a deletion inserted after candidate selection, partial KV failure, bounded resume/account batches, cursor revision conflicts, exact repository schemas/FK checks and UID-versus-numeric ownership. A signed checkout webhook fixture verifies retry and exactly-once fulfillment after maintenance ends; Analytics has its own defer/retry case. CI also dry-run builds retention and executes its compiled cleanup function against real schemas and intercepted KV. These checks do not establish live cleanup, provider deletion or readiness to apply migration 028.


## Bounded inactivity warnings and scheduled recovery

The old direct Firebase deletion, best-effort KV/D1 erasure, plan-label repair and warning-without-address paths are removed. The replacement hourly worker defaults to audit, has no HTTP execution endpoint, resumes at most five saved requests and inspects at most five new candidates per invocation. A revisioned candidate cursor advances/wraps only for complete batches; scoped tests stay on their selected account. Audit reports database candidates with provider checks explicitly unperformed and supports read-only pagination.

New work takes exclusive maintenance, checks fresh activity, verifies billing without mutations, then either sends an eligible 23-month warning or creates a 24-month inactivity intent. Its maintenance claim is settled before calling the shared processor so it cannot wait on itself. A confirmed warning receipt and the user's notice timestamp commit together. Missing email never produces a notice flag. A new activity period requires a new warning cycle.

Warning requests persist their sending state before the provider call and use stable payloads/idempotency keys. Definitive rejections allow at most three attempts; ambiguous responses, network failures, malformed receipts and receipt-persistence failures retain uncertainty and are not automatically resent. Overlapping runs cannot send again while the earlier operation is unresolved. Crashed claims/execution tokens are excluded from recovery instead of expiring. Scheduled incomplete/uncertain batches are reported as failed runs; this does not establish an owner-notification integration.

Development/QA execution requires one configured disposable UID. Their frontend currently selects the same Firebase project, so QA activation must not use an identity needed in development. The draft configuration pins the selected projects and matching frontend origins; no credential or remote configuration was changed. The [worker runbook](../workers/inactive-account-cleaner/README.md) documents activation requirements and the remaining reconciliation/notification/provider-coordination gates.

## Completion notification sender (draft, September 20)

The inactive-account worker now runs an independent completion outbox pass before recovery. Only completed jobs with matching completed admissions can dispatch. Atomic execution ownership prevents overlapping sends; definitive rejection retries are bounded to three with one/four-hour backoff and an identical versioned payload/key. Ambiguous responses, crashes and failed receipt persistence retain the token without automatic takeover or resending. Provider acceptance means API acceptance, not inbox delivery.

Acceptance clears the address immediately. Unsent addresses become eligible for redaction at seven days and address-free terminal notification receipts for purging at 90 days, in batches of 100 each; expiry backlog is reported and fails an executing scheduled run until cleared. A late provider receipt can settle without restoring a redacted address. Audit remains read-only. Failure never reopens erasure or recreates user content, and completed recovery never restages a purged notice. These retention periods do not delete operational UID receipts or existing anti-abuse tombstones. No real email, migration or deployment was performed.

Stopped, uncertain deliveries can be suppressed without representing them as delivered. Durable receipts block resends even after a marker/outbox reset; financial state and Google verification remain unchanged. Billing cannot use a delivery/storage disposition. See [the operation procedure](account-operation-reconciliation.md). This provides the draft recovery procedure for unfinished claims; real invocation/provider verification and disposable-account QA remain required, alongside the external-writer/live-session and runtime gates.

## Managed voice-call control (draft, not connected to the browser yet)

A Realtime client secret can create multiple sessions until it expires; an already-started session can continue afterward. Live deployments still use that credential flow. The draft browser now selects managed signalling when `/api/plan/me` returns `voice.transport: managed`. Neither a completed local report nor the app's 20-minute timer proves that a provider call has ended. The previously deployed browser cancellation fix remains separate.

Migration028 now adds a provider-call ledger without user/history cascade deletion. The new internal call helper exchanges SDP through the server using the existing gpt-realtime-mini/marin configuration, saves the call ID returned in OpenAI's Location header before returning an answer, and never stores API keys, SDP, prompts, transcripts or audio. A conditional insert and partial unique index prevent competing live calls for one interview. Ownership, deletion admission and maintenance claims gate creation. No reusable provider credential is returned by this helper.

Hangup accepts an internal attempt ID and verified owner; it looks up the provider reference rather than accepting a call ID from the browser. A hash of the issuing key detects unexpected provider credential changes. Each close has an exclusive durable execution. A 404, timeout, malformed create response, lost receipt or crash remains unresolved; no elapsed lease silently clears it. Definite create rejection allows a fresh attempt. The API does not document every already-closed-call error, so those outcomes need actual QA/provider evidence before release.

The deletion processor now checks tracked calls before billing/identity/content changes and closes at most one per retry. It owns the recovery job first. Automatic inactivity deletion rechecks returning-user activity before attempting a hangup, so it can withdraw the deletion intent while leaving the interview intact. Unknown calls preserve the account and return a stable pending reference. Manifest preparation is allowed while a call is outstanding; identity/content phases require full quiescence. The final erasure transaction also refuses unresolved calls and removes closed call metadata with the account.

The new authenticated `POST /api/voice/connection` route supports open/reconnect and durable close requests. It is dark unless both `VOICE_INTERVIEW_ENABLED` and the separate `VOICE_MANAGED_CALLS_ENABLED` equal `true`. The latter is not configured remotely. Request bodies are bounded while reading. Only verified authentication supplies the owner; body-supplied user/provider IDs are ignored. Provider work is registered with `waitUntil` before dispatch and remains inside account admission even when the client HTTP signal aborts. No provider credentials or authoritative provider call IDs are returned.

The route now uses a managed interview lifecycle: provider setup precedes the atomic credit/history reservation, and the answer is withheld until reservation and a fresh close/deletion check succeed. Failed reservations close the known call. A lost database receipt preserves the existing session ID for recovery without automatic refunds or a second charge. Reconnect requires the current application attempt ID, closes that owned call first and never consumes another entitlement. A stale reconnect cannot close a newer replacement. Reusable credentials are not part of this new route. When the cutover flag is enabled, the old `/api/voice/session` issuer returns a refresh-required conflict without minting a credential. This does not disable older deployments or previously issued credentials by itself.

Independent control records preserve End even when completion overtakes startup. A delayed create response cannot reserve credit after that intent, deletion intent or deadline expiry; the known call is closed instead. The same transaction stores reservation evidence, so erased history cannot turn the same ID into a second purchase. The managed subscription gate counts retained history and managed reservation receipts once each, including receipts whose history was erased, and rechecks subscription eligibility at the atomic insert. This does not reconstruct previously deleted legacy usage. The plan/history/report endpoints now select the managed counter with the same cutover flag; a missing control schema makes managed entitlements unavailable instead of advertising usable access.

The browser now obtains one cancellable microphone stream, posts its SDP to the authenticated app, and applies an answer only while that attempt still owns the connection. Failed starts keep their ID for credit-safe retry; an existing-call conflict records the owned application attempt and requires the next explicit retry to replace it. Reconnect carries the transcript and lifecycle context and retains the original deadline. Late streams and answers cannot reopen an ended interview. A tab-close warning also covers pending setup.

End during setup sends durable close intent immediately. An active interview first stops microphone/playback and allows the bounded final-transcript flush, then requests provider hangup while its peer is still open. The close request is bounded at the browser, and the completion route registers closure/transcript/scorecard work inside account admission. Completion re-reads the reservation after close intent: a never-reserved start can be cancelled without claiming a charge; erased previously reserved history cannot. Saved reports are idempotent and remain available even if closure is unconfirmed. A 202 distinguishes `saved` from `connectionClosed`; the browser keeps the exact completion payload and a finishing retry while separately displaying an acknowledged report. A later failed request cannot relabel an already-saved transcript as unsaved.

Closing an older untracked session records a persistent `legacy_unverified` hold rather than inventing successful provider closure. Both deletion preflight and final erasure refuse this hold. The draft now requires an acknowledged per-interview Durable Object alarm before provider creation/replacement. The alarm persists close intent and hangs up an exclusively claimed active call at the original deadline, independently of the browser. Storage errors and in-flight attempts retain an observation alarm; unknown provider results require review and are never automatically repeated. End/alarm races share the same D1 close claim. Disabling new schedules preserves existing alarms. See the [deadline worker runbook](../workers/voice-deadlines/README.md) for configuration and failure behavior. Alarms can be delayed by outages; this is not a verified exact wall-clock guarantee.

**Release integration is unfinished.** Do not deploy this draft as a complete call-control solution or enable its cutover flag. Browser/completion/entitlement and deadline-alarm integration are implemented and covered by intercepted-provider tests; they are not live QA acceptance. The deadline worker is not deployed or bound and its issuing key is not configured. Remaining work: provide reviewed recovery for unknown attempts, alarm receipts and legacy holds; disable/drain old credential issuers, credentials and deployments at cutover; validate actual creation/hangup/error semantics, alarm execution and human voice behavior in QA. Migration028 remains unapplied. Existing operation/job reconciliation cannot by itself clear a provider-call or legacy hold.
