## Practice the follow-up

LinkedIn: `93a7a772-abb3-4e8d-af33-4e38d8869289`

Pick one interview question and answer it out loud. Then ask yourself: What did I personally decide? Why did I choose that approach? What happened next?

If the follow-up is harder to answer, you have something specific to work on. Add the missing detail, then try the answer again. Keep it true to what you actually did.

## Make your contribution clear

LinkedIn: `d30f0a8d-4571-44a7-8e7a-ac61883b701d`

After a practice answer, write one sentence that explains your contribution.

For example: “I kept the launch schedule current and followed up with each team when a deadline slipped.” This is an illustrative example. Use your own work and be clear about what you led or supported.

Now listen to your answer. Does it explain that contribution, or does it mostly describe what the team did? Add one decision you made and what happened next.

## Tell me about yourself: three prompts

LinkedIn: `a877ca3a-133b-4c8b-b338-9560d1cbfaf4`

Preparing for “Tell me about yourself”? Start with three prompts.

What do you do now?
What is one relevant example from your experience?
Why does this role interest you?

Keep the job description beside you. Pick two responsibilities and check whether your introduction explains how your experience connects to them. Then say it out loud.

For this exercise, try roughly a minute. Listen for background you can cut and useful detail you left out. Let the wording change on the next attempt. Keep the facts consistent.

## Turn the job posting into a practice sheet

Instagram: `22a78838-9c38-48ea-8eff-05d0e58705a3`

Open the job posting and make three columns.

The role: pick two or three responsibilities.
Your experience: write one example for each, including what you personally led or supported.
Your introduction: use one relevant example to explain what you do now and why this role interests you.

Try your introduction out loud. Listen for missing detail or background you can cut. Keep the facts consistent on the next attempt.

## The 90-second answer check

LinkedIn: `024ac253-1173-461d-8311-2d8d244c4c95`

Try this before your next interview. Pick one question and record a 90-second answer on your phone. The timer is just a practice constraint.

Listen back. Did you answer the question? Did you give a specific example? Did you explain your own contribution?

Choose one thing to change and record a second attempt. Compare the two. Keep the version that explains your experience more clearly.
